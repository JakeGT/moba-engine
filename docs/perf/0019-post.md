Every tier stays far inside its budget on this GPU. Scaling the 2560×1380 rows by pixels
(×1.05 to 2560×1440, ×2.35 to 3840×2160) gives about 0.40 ms for High at 1440p and 1.3 ms

This does not close the §7 targets, which are defined on the reference GPUs (still unowned,
#9/#127): it shows the post chain's share of the frame and that no pass is out of scale.

**Reading:**
- **What R6 costs, by pass (1080p → 2560×1380):** bloom (five 13-tap down-samples, four tent
  up-samples) 0.039–0.041 → 0.053–0.061 ms; FXAA 0.016 (fast, Low) / 0.021 → 0.026 / 0.032 ms;
  the composite grows by 0.004–0.007 ms for the LUT, the bloom tap and the effects; GTAO and
  its blur at half resolution 0.046 (2 slices) / 0.059 (3 slices) → 0.091 / 0.116 ms;
  the exposure pass 0.003–0.004 ms; the Low upscale 0.010 → 0.016 ms.
- **The depth prepass pays for itself without MSAA:** High's prepass is 0.008–0.011 ms and
  its scene pass gets cheaper (0.066 → 0.063, 0.111 → 0.104 ms), because the scene shades
  each pixel once against the prepass depth. With MSAA the prepass costs more (0.032 /
  0.050 ms: four depth samples per pixel) but still saves part of the scene's cost (compare
  `ultra-msaa-noao`'s scene, below).
- **Low's scene pass is 0.004 ms slower** (0.050 → 0.054 ms), most likely the AO tap every
  scene fragment now makes (a white 1×1 image when AO is off). A permutation without the tap
  is not worth a pipeline per tier at this cost.
- The `p50 range` is within ±0.01 ms of the median except for four rounds that ran during
  other agents' GPU work (Low 1080p after, High 2560×1380 before, Ultra 2560×1380 after,
  Low 2560×1380 before); the medians are unaffected.

**Anti-aliasing, and why the tier defaults are what they are:**

| AA on High | 1080p total | 2560×1380 total | Added VRAM (1080p / 1440p / 4K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| off (`high-aaoff`) | 0.214 ms | 0.348 ms | — |
| FXAA (High default) | 0.234 ms (+0.020) | 0.379 ms (+0.031) | 8 / 15 / 33 MB (the LDR image) |
| MSAA 4× (`high-msaa`) | 0.309 ms (+0.095) | 0.502 ms (+0.154) | 100 / 177 / 398 MB |

MSAA 4× costs about five times what FXAA costs on this GPU and twelve times the memory, and
its cost is bandwidth: the scene pass writes four colour and depth samples, which is what the
Medium/High reference GPUs (GTX 1060 / RTX 3060 class, 192-bit buses and 6–12 GB) have least
of. It is also the only option that keeps thin geometry and distant minions stable, which is
why the RTX 4070-class Ultra tier takes it. FXAA's 0.02–0.03 ms buys most of the edge
smoothing for every tier. `ultra-fxaa` (0.272 / 0.424 ms) is the Ultra frame with FXAA, for a
user who prefers the memory. Medium with MSAA (`medium-msaa`, no prepass) is the worst case:
its scene pass shades without the prepass's early-Z (0.130 / 0.253 ms at 1080p / 2560×1380,
against 0.114 / 0.207 ms for High with MSAA after its prepass).

**Ambient occlusion:** AO and its prepass add 0.045 ms at 1080p and 0.087 ms at 2560×1380 on
High (`high-aooff`: 0.189 / 0.292 ms), mostly GTAO itself. **Bloom** adds 0.016 / 0.028 ms
(`high-bloomoff`); with bloom off the down-chain still runs on Medium and up because the
tier allows eye adaptation (it meters the last mip: 0.023 / 0.033 ms), and
`MOBA_EXPOSURE=fixed` removes it. **Eye adaptation** itself costs nothing measurable
(`high-auto`: the exposure pass runs either way). **Render scale 0.7** cuts High's GPU total by
23–31 % (0.234 → 0.180, 0.379 → 0.261 ms) including the 0.026 / 0.043 ms bicubic upscale;
Low at 0.5 cuts its scene pass to under a third (0.054 → 0.016 ms) and its total to 0.072 ms.

## Lavapipe (a slow-device stand-in) and dynamic resolution

The same replay on lavapipe at 1280×720 (two rounds of Low and High, before and after, plus
Low with dynamic resolution and at scale 0.7) did **not** give usable before/after numbers:
lavapipe rasterises on the CPU, which three other agents were loading with builds, and the
same configuration differed by up to 4× between rounds (Low after: 24.4 ms, then 5.5 ms GPU
total; High before: 24.5 ms, then 6.8 ms). What it does show:

- **Dynamic resolution engages and holds the target.** With Low's defaults (14 ms GPU target,
  scale 0.6–1.0) the loaded round measured 17.4 ms at p50 and the controller settled at
  **920×518** (scale 0.72), while the quiet round stayed at 1280×720 (5.8 ms). Its rules
  (EMA, a 15-frame hold, `sqrt(target / measured)` steps down above 95 % of the target and
  +0.05 below 75 %, 1/32 quantisation) are unit-tested in `crates/render/src/dynres.rs`.
- **On a software rasteriser every fullscreen pass costs about as much as the composite**
  (FXAA 1.3–1.8 ms, the upscale 0.6 ms in the quiet round), so on Low the fixed-cost passes
  matter more than on a GPU: bloom and AO are off there, and the Low upscale runs only
  because dynamic resolution is on (`MOBA_DYNRES=off` removes it at scale 1). Measuring Low
  on a UHD 620 / M1 (#9/#127) is what will decide whether Low keeps FXAA.

Protocol (about ten minutes per rater; at least three raters who have not seen this note's key
or the PR's images):

1. Show each pair full-screen on a calibrated-enough desktop monitor at 100 % zoom, in the
   order above, for at most 20 s each. Do not say which build is which or what changed.
2. For each pair the rater answers: (a) *Which side makes it easier to tell red units from
   blue units at a glance?* (b) *On which side are the minions, heroes and towers easier to
   pick out from the ground?* (c) *Is anything on either side harder to read — smeared,
   washed out, too dark, coloured wrong?* (free text). "No difference" is a valid answer.
3. Record answers per pair as A / B / same. Then reveal the key below.
4. **Pass:** for (a) and (b), R6 is chosen or "same" in at least 5 of 6 pairs by every rater,
   and no (c) remark names an R6 side for a team-colour or unit-visibility problem. Any (c)
   remark against R6 becomes an issue (a grade, bloom strength or AO radius to tune, per map
   in `[post]` or per tier in `PostConfig::for_tier`) before sign-off.
